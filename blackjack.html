What is counting worth?

Edge on money wagered is the player's average profit per round divided by the amount bet: +1% means winning one cent per dollar bet, on average. It is the comparable number between rows. Win rate per round is in units of the minimum bet, so it also reflects how much is bet. Sitting out means not playing at all when the count or edge is negative, which real tables may not allow. Ramps: Hi-Lo bets 1 unit below true count +2, then 2, 4, 8, 16 at +2, +3, +4, +5; Ace-Five doubles the last bet at a count of +2 or more and resets to 1 unit below; the perfect player scales linearly from 1 unit at zero edge to the maximum at +1.5%. Bankroll unlimited, no table maximum, heads-up.

What if you could see cards you should not?

Same rules and engine, 1M rounds per row, flat bets unless stated, other seats at a four-player table playing basic strategy. A player who sees the next card off the shoe, the dealer's hole card, or both, and plays each hand by expected value with that knowledge. "Differs from basic" is the share of decisions where the chart answer was overridden. Both powers correspond to real cheating methods that casinos prosecute; this is a thought experiment.

Why the seat matters: at the last seat, or heads-up, the card you decline goes to the dealer, so standing can feed the dealer a bust card. In a middle seat it goes to the next player instead. Hole-card knowledge does not depend on the seat because the dealer's hand is fixed before anyone acts. Heads-up, the next card off the shoe is your own first card, so you know it before betting; the last row bets 8 units when it is an ace or ten.

…and tried not to get caught?

The plays above would be spotted within a shoe: nobody hits a 19. Here the same player, heads-up with flat bets, only deviates to a play that looks ordinary: one whose EV without the power is within δ of the basic-strategy best. At δ = 0.02 that means choosing only between near coin-flips like 16 vs 10 or 12 vs 4; at δ = 0.20 it allows anything a loose player might do, such as hitting any stiff hand. Insurance follows the ordinary count rule.

Which cards are your friends?

Effect of removal: take a fresh 6-deck shoe (S17, DAS, late surrender), remove one card of a rank, and recompute the exact basic-strategy edge. A card whose removal hurts you is one you want left in the shoe, so the top of the table is on your side and the bottom works for the house. Small cards help the dealer because the dealer must hit every stiff 12 to 16 and never doubles; a five turns any stiff into a made hand without busting. Big cards bust those stiffs, pay off your doubles and make your 3:2 blackjacks. Note that 7 is a dealer card and 9 a player card, both rounded to zero by Hi-Lo, and that Ace-Five keeps just the two extremes.

Dealer's final hand by up card

Fresh shoe, current rules. For an ace or ten up, the dealer has already peeked and does not have blackjack. This is why you stand on stiff hands against 2 to 6 and hit them against 7 to ace.
